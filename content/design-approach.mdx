I’ve tried every diamond, squiggle and loop diagram to explain my design process, but real projects are far messier. So instead of showing some abstract diagram you’ve seen before, here’s how I really work.

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I ask “why?” and “what if?” (a lot)

I start by questioning, framing, and looking at problems from different angles to make sure we’re aiming at the right thing.

- I start with benchmarks and a clear picture of success.
- I work closely with User Researchers to plan, conduct and analyse research.
- I use data, workshops, and competitor analysis to uncover questions and opportunities.
- I look across causes, effects, processes and systems, using frameworks like Cynefin and Stacey to classify problems.
- I synthesise data and insights into permanent, digestible, memorable things.

</ApproachPrinciple>

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I design with people (and pens)

I believe the best design comes from working inclusively and collaboratively, bringing in diverse voices and keeping ideas light and flexible.

- I design inclusively and prioritise accessibility over aesthetics.
- I collaborate to leverage diverse knowledge, create shared understanding, and build common language.
- I see co-design as a way to inform, not create, solutions.
- I consider the needs of workshop participants as much as the people we're designing for.
- I sketch to explore ideas freely, without attachment or tool constraints.
- I use heuristics, principles, and standards to guide quality.

</ApproachPrinciple>

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I prototype with intent

I treat prototyping as a way to communicate, test ideas and help teams move forward with confidence.

- I prototype to learn and communicate.
- I use Ruecker's model to understand a prototype's purpose.
- I use Houde and Hill's model to understand a prototype's focus.
- I use "fidelity" to refer to interaction, not form.
- I favour lots of small prototypes over building a complex system.
- I create prototypes using digital tools, code, Excel, paper, and roleplay.

</ApproachPrinciple>

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I test with users (usually)

I see user research as essential for learning, but understand its limits.

- I dislike when teams use a lack of research as a shield for decision-making.
- I see user research as a spectator sport: everyone should observe and learn.
- I often say “I’ve spent 20 years getting things wrong and fixing them.”
- I iterate prototypes during testing if we’ve clearly missed the mark.
- I ensure research findings are recorded and design decisions remain traceable.

</ApproachPrinciple>

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I empathise with development

I work closely with engineers, respecting their craft and constraints.

- I’m a “recovering software engineer” and still fluent in APIs, CI, and systems thinking.
- I involve inquisitive engineers in design as early as possible.
- I know ideas only become reality through pragmatic problem solving and iteration.
- I respect why engineers value consistency, clarity, and long-term vision.
- I can’t be fooled by “that’s not technically possible” (many have tried).

</ApproachPrinciple>

<ApproachPrinciple>

### I learn when it’s live

Perfect is the enemy of done. It only counts when it’s live.

</ApproachPrinciple>
