# <b>Context</b>

I was the lead UX Designer for the re-design, development and delivery of a large, business-critical, Government service. The service is used by 150,000 internal users who perform over 40 million transactions a year.

The project was delivered using dual-track Agile with 5 Scrum teams located in Nottingham, Gdansk and Belfast.

<b>Design challenge</b>

<b>While the service's personas were regularly referenced by the research and design team, attempts to get the product and delivery teams to use them failed.</b>

It was clear our personas weren't working. Teams regularly mocked the back stories, failing to see their relevance, and joked about the stock photos (they really sucked). As a result, the original personas were left to rot in confluence, leaving teams without a common understanding of our users.

<b>Team</b>

UX Designer.

<b>Timescale</b>

1 week (part-time) for research analysis and visual re-design. Note: The original personas were based on the findings of over 200 user interviews.

# <b>Approach</b>

While I don't normally advocate polished personas, I was aware of the original personas' failings. I decided to simplify the content and give them a visual refresh.

Changes included:

- Aggressively editing content to make them more accessible and readable
- Removing backstories as the narrative distracted product and delivery teams
- Replacing the original stock imagery with high-quality photos to add credibility

All changes were validated by the project's user research community.

# <b>Outcome</b>

The re-design was successful. The refreshed, now credible, personas were adopted by teams and referenced throughout the design and development process.
