## Overview

Working within a small consulting team, we were asked to help NHS Digital's product teams improve their service design practices.

### Challenge

<Challenge>

### To help NHS Digital's teams understand their services within the context of the larger digital ecosystem.

</Challenge>

While teams were using a robust user-centred design process to create digital services and content, the organisation had concerns the end-to-end user journeys were suffering as a result of team silos.

For example, while one team was responsible for exploring possible solutions to “Help Type 1 Diabetes suffers manage their ongoing self-care” separate teams were responsible for:

- Symptoms content
- Conditions content
- Booking primary care appointments
- 111 Online
- Finding health services
- Electronic Prescriptions
- Electronic Referrals

All of which could be encountered during a user’s journey.

**Analysed the content ecosystem** using spidering and link analysis to help teams understand interdependencies

**Advised senior stakeholders** on scaling service design practices across teams and disciplines

**Timescale**: 3 months (2 weeks with each product team)

## Approach

## Key learnings

Service design adoption requires more than introducing new tools and templates—it’s about shifting mindsets. I learned the value of meeting teams where they are, using language and artefacts that resonate with their day-to-day work. The content link analysis, for example, turned out to be a powerful entry point - because it gave content teams something new, tangible and relevant to engage with.

I also saw how visualising journeys and systems side-by-side helps reveal tensions between how services are structured internally and how they’re experienced externally. 

Finally, I learned that long-term change in service design practice depends on both leadership buy-in and giving individual teams the confidence to act beyond their immediate scope.
